## Learn from the response

Stars can help people bookmark the repository, but they do not establish learning quality. Look for evidence that people can find the first lesson, run the example, understand the next step, and recover their progress.

GitHub's traffic view can show recent repository views, clones, and referring sites when you have the necessary access. Record a dated baseline before a launch and review it after one and two weeks. Traffic windows are limited, some referrers may be absent, and changes do not prove that a particular post caused the result. Keep a simple private record rather than adding analytics to the learning app solely for promotion.

Useful feedback prompts:

- “Which exact step first became confusing, and what did you expect to happen?”
- “Could you complete the independent exercise without copying the worked example?”
- “Which prerequisite did the lesson assume before explaining it?”
- “Did export and import preserve the work you expected?”

A modest invitation in the README is enough: “If this helps you learn, a star makes it easier to find again.” Let people decide whether it is useful. Do not trade stars, purchase engagement, automate promotional replies, or treat issue reports as a mailing list.
